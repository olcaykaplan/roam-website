It is easy to describe Florence through its museums: the Uffizi, the Ponte Vecchio, the Duomo. But the city's liveliest side is eaten standing on the curb. We lined up the food quests from Roam's Florence set into a single route: a sandwich, a pizza, two gelato stops and a sunset.

## 1. All'Antico Vinaio: order the Paradiso

Florence's most famous sandwich is built on warm schiacciata with stracciatella, mortadella and pistachio cream, and it is called the Paradiso. The queue looks long but moves fast. Eat it on the curb across the street with a glass of Chianti.

## 2. Gustapizza: pizza on the Santo Spirito steps

A tiny Oltrarno pizzeria where students queue out the door. Napoli-style wood-oven pizza; you can ask nicely for the heart-shaped margherita. Carry the box to the church steps in Piazza Santo Spirito and eat with the neighbourhood swirling around you. It is closed Mondays, so build the route around that.

## 3. La Carraia: gelato by the river

This local favourite by the Carraia bridge scoops silky gelato at prices that feel like a mistake. Limone-basilico or the namesake La Carraia cream are good choices. Eat on the bridge and watch the rowers slide under.

## 4. Vivoli: the oldest gelateria

Four generations of the Vivoli family have made gelato here since 1929. No cones, only cups, the old way. The flavor people cross the city for is riso (rice pudding). Order an affogato if you want the coffee too.

## 5. Ponte Vecchio: 30 minutes before sunset

Built in 1345, the bridge is described as the only Florentine bridge not destroyed in WWII, and the jewellery shops on it have been there for centuries. At dusk the light on the Arno turns everything gold. It is a good full stop after all that food.

## Don't forget the museum

If you want the Uffizi on the same day, set aside at least three hours and pre-book, or you may wait 90 minutes. Botticelli's Venus, Caravaggio's Medusa and Leonardo's Annunciation are inside. Opening days and hours can change, so check.
