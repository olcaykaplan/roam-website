Eating in Rome is easy. Eating **well** takes a bit of planning. Skip the fluorescent gelato mountains and the picture menus around the Trevi Fountain. We picked the food-focused quests from Roam's Rome set, each one a place a local would actually go.

## 1. Da Remo, Testaccio: Roman pizza is not Neapolitan

Rome's pizza is scrocchiarella: cracker-thin, blistered, no ceremony. Da Remo serves it on paper tablecloths. Queue with the locals, order a margherita and fried baccalà. It is open for dinner only, so arrive at opening.

## 2. Fatamorgana: inventive all-natural gelato

Kentucky tobacco, black rice, pear-gorgonzola. Everything at Fatamorgana is natural and seasonal. Ask to taste three flavors before you commit. It is worth skipping the brightly colored tourist mountains for this.

## 3. The gran caffè at Sant'Eustachio

One of Rome's most guarded coffee secrets since 1938: the first drops of espresso are whipped with sugar behind a screen so you can't see the technique. The result is a small cup of velvet. Stand at the bar like a Roman; sitting costs triple.

## 4. Bonci Pizzarium: pizza by the slice at its peak

Gabriele Bonci turned Roman pizza al taglio into an art form: 72-hour dough, bold toppings, sold by weight. The potato-and-mozzarella slice is the classic, eaten standing on the sidewalk near the Vatican. Order three different slices and avoid peak hours.

## 5. Da Enzo, Trastevere: the real cacio e pepe

Only a dozen tables and no reservations. Roman classics done exactly right: cacio e pepe, amatriciana, carbonara, tiramisù. Be there 30 minutes before opening, and get in line by 6:30pm or accept defeat.

## Timing at a glance

Most of this list says "go early": Da Remo at opening, Da Enzo half an hour before opening, Bonci off-peak. Opening hours can change, so check current information before you plan. If you have a gap between meals, see [4 things that fit into 30 minutes in Rome](/en/blog/rome-in-30-minutes/).
