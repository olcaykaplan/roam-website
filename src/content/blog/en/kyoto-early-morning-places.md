By late morning, Kyoto's best-known sights turn into a different city: the same bamboo path, the same red gates, but seen through a wall of phone screens. The good news is that most of Kyoto's busiest stops are still quiet in the early hours. We went through the Kyoto quests on Roam one by one, and these are the 5 stops that reward an early alarm.

## 1. Arashiyama Bamboo Grove: before 7am

Towering bamboo on both sides, light filtering through, stalks creaking in the wind. By 9am the path is packed; at sunrise you can have it almost to yourself. Plan about 45 minutes and aim for first light. It is the "green cathedral" of our [Kyoto quests](/en/cities/kyoto/).

## 2. Ninna-ji: the UNESCO site most visitors skip

It sits near Kinkaku-ji yet draws far fewer people. Founded in 888 AD as an emperor's retirement home, it has a five-story pagoda, an imperial residence and gardens. Its late-blooming Omuro cherry trees are legendary. About 75 minutes is enough.

## 3. Ryōan-ji: opens at 8am, be among the first in

Fifteen stones in raked white gravel, arranged so that from any angle only fourteen are visible at once. Sit on the veranda and try anyway; that is the puzzle. Being one of the first few visitors through the gate is the only way to actually think about it. Allow 45 minutes.

## 4. Fushimi Inari: before 8am, and don't turn back

Thousands of vermilion torii gates climb the mountain. Most visitors turn around after 20 minutes, so keep walking. The higher you go, the fewer people you meet, and the summit offers a quiet view over Kyoto. Set aside 2 to 3 hours; this is the most strenuous stop on the list.

## 5. Nishiki Market: before noon

With 100+ stalls, this narrow covered street has been Kyoto's kitchen for about 400 years. Try tako tamago (octopus stuffed with quail egg), yuba (tofu skin), warabi mochi and fresh sashimi sticks. It is calmer before noon. One etiquette tip: don't eat while walking; stand by the stall.

## How to plan it

Arashiyama, Ninna-ji and Ryōan-ji are in the northwest of Kyoto and fairly close together, so they fit into one morning. Fushimi Inari is on the opposite side of the city and pairs better with Nishiki Market on a separate morning. Opening hours and fees can change, so check official pages before you go.
